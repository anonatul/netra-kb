# 1. Purpose

This document defines the stakeholders who interact with, provide information to, deploy, maintain, govern, or benefit from NETRA.

The goal is to answer:

* Who actually uses NETRA?
* Who provides data?
* Who makes decisions?
* Who benefits?
* Who owns the deployment?
* Why would each stakeholder adopt NETRA?
* Why might they reject it?
* What additional effort does NETRA create?
* What must be validated before deployment?

# 3. Primary User — Emergency Operations Centre

## Role

The Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) / emergency control room is the most important NETRA user.

The control room is responsible for developing situational awareness and coordinating response activities.

The exact organizational structure varies by state, district and disaster-response setup and must be validated with real operators.

---

## Current Workflow

A simplified workflow is:

```text
Incoming information
        ↓
Receive / monitor reports
        ↓
Identify incident
        ↓
Assess severity
        ↓
Determine location
        ↓
Coordinate resources
        ↓
Track response
        ↓
Close / update incident
```

Actual workflows may involve multiple systems and human operators.

NETRA must not assume that every agency follows an identical process.

---

## Current Pain Points

Potential problems include:

* high report volume
* duplicate reports
* unstructured messages
* varying information quality
* location uncertainty
* stale information
* difficult prioritization
* limited operator attention
* fragmented information sources
* difficulty maintaining a current operational picture

These are hypotheses that should be validated through responder interviews or documented workflows.

---

## Why They Would Use NETRA

NETRA should provide:

* prioritized incident queue
* consolidated incident clusters
* evidence visibility
* location confidence
* severity information
* vulnerability information
* incident freshness
* resource recommendations
* map-based situational awareness
* audit history

The core benefit is:

> **Reduce cognitive workload while improving the quality and speed of operational prioritization.**

---

## Why They Might Reject NETRA

Potential objections:

* "We already have an emergency management system."
* "Our existing GIS already shows incidents."
* "AI recommendations cannot be trusted."
* "Another dashboard increases workload."
* "We don't have staff to operate another system."
* "Integration with existing systems is difficult."
* "The system may generate false alarms."
* "Who is responsible if NETRA recommends the wrong response?"
* "The system is unavailable during network failure."
* "The data is not sufficiently reliable."

These objections must be addressed through evidence, not marketing.

---

## Adoption Requirement

NETRA must:

* fit into existing workflows
* avoid duplicate data entry
* integrate with existing systems where possible
* provide explainable recommendations
* allow human override
* preserve access to original evidence
* operate with low training requirements
* support offline/local operation where required
* provide authentication and audit logs

---

# 4. Primary User — Rescue Teams

Examples may include:

* NDRF
* SDRF
* police rescue personnel
* fire and emergency services
* trained local rescue teams
* medical response teams

The exact operational workflow varies by organization.

---

## What They Need

Rescue teams generally need actionable information rather than raw reports.

NETRA should help answer:

```text id="7tlfx2"
WHERE?
   ↓
HOW URGENT?
   ↓
HOW MANY?
   ↓
WHO IS VULNERABLE?
   ↓
CAN WE REACH THEM?
   ↓
WHAT RESOURCES MAY BE REQUIRED?
```

---

## Useful NETRA Information

A rescue team should be able to see:

```text id="gjdrqv"
Zone:
Alpha-17

Priority:
P1 Critical

Location confidence:
94%

Estimated victims:
32

Critical:
5

Vulnerable:
9

Last corroborated signal:
2 minutes

Access:
Road blocked

Recommended resources:
2 rescue boats
1 medical team
```

The recommendation should remain decision support rather than an automatic order.

---

## Why They Would Use NETRA

Potential benefits:

* better route/zone selection
* reduced time spent searching for incidents
* clearer understanding of victim needs
* reduced duplicate reporting
* better resource preparation
* awareness of changing incident priority

---

## Why They Might Reject NETRA

* inaccurate coordinates
* outdated information
* excessive alerts
* over-reliance on AI
* poor field connectivity
* unfamiliar interface
* recommendations that don't match field reality
* lack of integration with existing command structure

---

## Adoption Requirement

NETRA must allow field teams to:

* verify an incident
* update incident status
* mark an incident as rescued
* report new conditions
* reject incorrect information
* provide field observations
* work with limited connectivity

# 5. Decision Maker — Emergency Commander

The emergency commander is responsible for allocating limited resources across competing incidents.

This is where NETRA's priority engine becomes operationally important.

---

## Decision Problem

Suppose:

```text id="4i9ryu"
Zone A:
50 people
low medical urgency

Zone B:
20 people
4 critical patients
road blocked

Zone C:
30 people
signals are 2 hours old
```

The commander needs to determine which resources should move first.

NETRA should provide evidence for the decision rather than simply declaring:

> "Zone B is correct."

---

## Why They Would Use NETRA

* strategic situational awareness
* priority overview
* resource planning
* incident evolution
* unresolved incident monitoring
* evidence-backed prioritization

---

## Why They Might Reject NETRA

* scoring methodology unclear
* AI not explainable
* inaccurate input data
* inability to override recommendations
* legal/accountability concerns
* lack of integration with command systems

---

## Required Design Principle

Every priority score must be explainable.

Example:

```text id="whv0h4"
P1 because:
+ high severity
+ vulnerable victims
+ recent evidence
+ high location confidence
+ blocked access
```

---

# 6. Stakeholder — District Administration

District authorities may require a broader situation view.

---

## Needs

* affected areas
* active incidents
* resource requirements
* unresolved zones
* response progress
* high-level situation reports

---

## NETRA Value

NETRA can transform detailed incident information into an operational summary.

Example:

```text id="11h7k2"
District Status

Active incidents: 418

P1: 27
P2: 91
P3: 300

Estimated affected:
8,430

Medical-critical:
183

Unresolved high-confidence zones:
31
```

These numbers are examples only and must not be presented as real disaster statistics.

---

## Adoption Risk

District administration may already have:

* command dashboards
* GIS platforms
* emergency reporting systems
* manual reporting processes

Therefore NETRA must demonstrate **incremental value**, not merely provide another visualization.

---

# 7. Stakeholder — Citizens

Citizens are primarily:

> **Data providers and beneficiaries, not the primary NETRA dashboard users.**

This distinction is important.

---

## Citizen Problem

During a disaster, citizens may face:

* lack of connectivity
* stress
* limited technical ability
* inability to install an app
* low battery
* uncertain location
* language barriers
* inability to provide detailed information

---

## NETRA Design Goal

Minimize citizen interaction requirements.

The ideal flow is:

```text id="j9f7ro"
Existing emergency channel
        ↓
Simple distress report
        ↓
NETRA processing
        ↓
Responder action
```

---

## Why Citizens Would Use It

If NETRA improves existing emergency channels, citizens benefit indirectly through:

* faster identification
* better prioritization
* reduced duplicate handling
* better communication of victim needs

---

## Why Citizens Might Reject It

* privacy concerns
* fear of tracking
* inability to send messages
* uncertainty about whether reports are received
* language barriers
* distrust
* false expectations

---

## Critical Principle

NETRA must never imply:

> "If you send a message, rescue is guaranteed."

The system is decision support operating inside a larger emergency-response ecosystem.

---

# 8. Stakeholder — Field Responders

Field responders can provide highly valuable evidence because they observe real conditions.

Examples:

* police officers
* rescue personnel
* firefighters
* medical teams
* authorized disaster workers

---

## Potential Inputs

```text id="knq8zj"
Incident verified
Victim count updated
Road blocked
Water level increased
Victims rescued
Medical support required
False report
New incident discovered
```

---

## NETRA Opportunity

Field verification can increase incident confidence.

Example:

```text id="v0ocjv"
Citizen reports:
12

Independent sources:
8

Field confirmation:
YES

Location confidence:
HIGH

Incident confidence:
HIGH
```

This is more useful than treating every citizen report equally.

# 9. Stakeholder — Telecom Operators

Telecom operators are not ordinary NETRA users.

They may become:

> **Infrastructure/integration stakeholders.**

Potential roles:

* emergency SMS infrastructure
* location services
* network availability information
* emergency-service integration
* authorized disaster communication mechanisms

---

## Why They Matter

NETRA's ability to use communication signals depends on existing telecom infrastructure and authorized integrations.

---

## Adoption Concerns

* security
* privacy
* regulatory requirements
* API availability
* network reliability
* integration cost
* operational responsibility
* authentication
* lawful access

---

## Important Boundary

NETRA should not assume that a telecom operator will expose network-level capabilities.

Any such capability must be treated as:

> **Integration dependency / future deployment requirement**

until officially validated.

# 14. User vs Beneficiary

NETRA must explicitly distinguish:

### Primary Users

People who operate the NETRA interface:

* control-room operators
* emergency commanders
* authorized responders

### Data Providers

People/systems that generate information:

* citizens
* emergency channels
* field teams
* authorized external systems

### Beneficiaries

People who ultimately benefit:

* disaster-affected citizens
* responders
* administrators
* public services

### Integrators

Organizations needed for production integration:

* government technology systems
* telecom operators
* emergency-service infrastructure

This distinction should remain consistent throughout the project.

# 15. Adoption Equation

NETRA will only be adopted if:

```text id="umxbyl"
Perceived Operational Benefit
>
Integration Cost
+
Training Cost
+
Workflow Friction
+
Risk
```

A technically useful system can still fail if the adoption burden is too high.

Therefore NETRA should minimize:

* new applications
* new data-entry requirements
* duplicate workflows
* manual configuration
* training requirements
* dependence on new infrastructure

# 18. Stakeholder Validation Plan

Before making strong adoption claims, NETRA should obtain evidence from:

### Minimum target

* 2–3 disaster-response/control-room workflow discussions
* 2–3 responder interviews
* 1 emergency-management expert review
* review of official emergency-response documentation
* usability test with representative operators if possible

If direct government interviews are impossible, use:

* official SOPs
* government RFPs
* emergency-system documentation
* published disaster-management reports
* research literature

Clearly label secondary evidence as secondary evidence.

# 19. Primary Persona — Control Room Operator

### Objective

Identify and prioritize unresolved emergency incidents quickly.

### Inputs

* emergency reports
* incident updates
* location information
* field verification

### Pain

Too much heterogeneous information and limited attention.

### Desired output

```text id="g9w1jb"
Top priority incidents
+
Reason
+
Confidence
+
Location
+
Victim information
+
Recommended response
```

### NETRA success condition

> The operator can identify the most important unresolved incidents faster and with less cognitive effort than with raw reports alone.

---

# 20. Primary Persona — Rescue Commander

### Objective

Allocate limited resources to the highest-priority operational needs.

### Pain

Multiple simultaneous incidents compete for limited resources.

### Desired output

```text id="2t5j3j"
Priority
+
Evidence
+
Location
+
Victim needs
+
Access constraints
+
Available resource requirement
```

### NETRA success condition

> The commander can understand the rationale behind NETRA's prioritization and make a faster informed decision.

---

# 21. Secondary Persona — Field Responder

### Objective

Verify and act on incidents.

### Pain

Information received from command may be stale or incomplete.

### Desired output

* incident location
* current status
* victim details
* access information
* ability to update status

### NETRA success condition

> Field observations can update the operational picture with minimal effort.

---

# 22. Stakeholder Design Principle

> **NETRA should fit into the responder's existing decision workflow rather than force responders to adopt a completely new emergency workflow.**

This principle should guide:

* UI
* integrations
* authentication
* data ingestion
* alerts
* field reporting
* deployment

---

# 23. Final Stakeholder Insight

The most important user is **not the citizen sending the message**.

The most important user is:

> **the authorized responder who must decide what to do with the information.**

Citizens provide evidence.

NETRA transforms evidence.

Responders make decisions.

That distinction should remain central to the product.

---

# 24. Stakeholder Memory Model

NETRA's stakeholder relationship can be remembered as:

```text id="g6i8k4"
CITIZEN
  ↓
Provides evidence

EXISTING EMERGENCY SYSTEM
  ↓
Provides authorized signals

NETRA
  ↓
Fuses + interprets + prioritizes

CONTROL ROOM
  ↓
Reviews + decides

RESCUE TEAM
  ↓
Acts + verifies

FIELD DATA
  ↓
Feeds back into NETRA
```

This creates a continuous operational loop:

```text id="7t3tzu"
REPORT
  ↓
UNDERSTAND
  ↓
PRIORITIZE
  ↓
RESPOND
  ↓
VERIFY
  ↓
UPDATE
  ↓
REPRIORITIZE
```

---

# 25. Final Stakeholder Principle

> **NETRA succeeds only if it improves the decisions of real responders without creating more work than it removes.**

Use this as the ultimate adoption test for every future feature.
